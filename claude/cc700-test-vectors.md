# CC700 test vectors

Version 1.1 (matches spec 0.5). Generated by a reference implementation (`ref.py`, included in the repo under `tools/reference/`) and self-checked before publishing. Every number below is exact. Tests in the firmware, Android app and tools must reproduce them bit for bit. If an implementation disagrees with this file, the implementation is wrong unless the vector is shown to contradict the spec, in which case raise it in `QUESTIONS.md`.

Changes in 1.1: W1's Recover step is now the range 45 to 55 % (it matches the coach example, which now keeps recovery inside one zone), so the W1 object, its ID, CRC and the framing bytes in section 5 changed. Added a 300 % row to 2.4, a floor-division check to 2.5, a 300 % accept case to 4.1, power bins (6.4) and the pill at step starts and on short steps (7.1).

Conventions: all multi-byte integers little-endian. Percentages are stored as integer tenths of a percent (`p10`; 55.0 % is 550). Rounding is half-up on integers: `(a*b + d/2) // d`. **`//` is floor division throughout.** C `/` and Kotlin `/` truncate toward zero and give a different answer when the numerator is negative (descending ramps, 2.5); ports must use a floor-division helper (`cc_floordiv` in C, `Math.floorDiv` in Kotlin). No floating point on the unit for anything in this file.

### 2.5 Ramp targets

Target at elapsed `e` seconds: `p(e) = p0 + ((p1-p0)*e*2 + secs) // (2*secs)` (floor). The step range at that instant is `p*95` to `p*105` (same widening), then watts. W1 warm-up (50 to 70 % over 600 s) and cool-down (55 to 40 % over 600 s), FTP 260:

## 3. Zones

## 5. Link framing

Packet = 1 header byte + data. Header: bit 7 START, bit 6 END, bits 5 to 0 packet counter (0 at START, +1 each packet, modulo 64). A message is `opcode, req_id, payload`. Data per packet is `ATT_MTU - 3 - 1` bytes at most: 19 at the default MTU of 23, 243 at an MTU of 247.

| message | bytes (opcode, req_id, payload) | packets at 19 B data | packets at 243 B data |
|---|---|---|---|
| HELLO request (phone) | `01 01 01` | 1 | 1 |
| HELLO response (unit): status 0, proto 1, fw 0.1.0, battery 68, state idle, 13 workout and 7 route slots free | `81 01 00 01 00 01 00 44 00 0d 07` | 1 | 1 |
| FTP_SET 260 (phone) | `03 02 04 01` | 1 | 1 |
| FTP_SET response: OK | `83 02 00` | 1 | 1 |
| OBJ_PUT_BEGIN for W1: kind 1, id, size, crc | `11 03 01 18 12 24 0e 18 09 c0 11 e3 00 00 00 8e 1a 20 09` | 1 | 1 |
| OBJ_PUT_END | `13 05` | 1 | 1 |

OBJ_PUT_DATA carrying all of W1 at offset 0 is a 233-byte message: 13 packets at the default MTU, 1 at 247. At the default MTU the packet headers are:

```
80 01 02 03 04 05 06 07 08 09 0a 0b 4c
```

First two packets and the last:

```
80 12 04 00 00 00 00 43 57 01 00 18 12 24 0e 18 09 c0 11 f0
01 0f 00 00 0a 13 34 20 78 20 38 20 6d 69 6e 20 74 68 72 65
4c 20 64 6f 77 6e
```

Unit reply to that data message: `92 04 00 e3 00 00 00` (OK, next offset 227). BAD_CRC case: change the byte at offset 100 of the object; the CRC-32 becomes `F5C8CCE3` and OBJ_PUT_END returns status 1.

A full 4000-byte OBJ_PUT_DATA chunk (protocol 4) is a 4006-byte message: 17 packets at an MTU of 247. The phone uses 4000-byte chunks only at an MTU of 185 or more, and 512-byte chunks otherwise.

GATT UUIDs: service `c7a1e6b0-4d52-4f0a-9c3e-0b5d7a2f8e11`, RX (phone writes, with or without response) `c7a1e6b1-4d52-4f0a-9c3e-0b5d7a2f8e11`, TX (unit notifies) `c7a1e6b2-4d52-4f0a-9c3e-0b5d7a2f8e11`.

## 6. Sensor parsing

### 6.1 Cycling Power Measurement (0x2A63)

Fields in order: flags u16, instantaneous power s16, then (if flag set) pedal balance u8 [bit 0], accumulated torque u16 [bit 2], wheel revolutions u32 + last wheel event time u16 [bit 4], crank revolutions u16 + last crank event time u16 (1/1024 s) [bit 5]. Anything beyond is ignored. Negative power is clamped to 0 by the caller.

| case | bytes | power | crank (revs, time) |
|---|---|---|---|
| crank data only | `20 00 c8 00 0a 00 00 04` | 200 | (10, 1024) |
| pedal balance and crank data | `21 00 c8 00 32 0a 00 00 04` | 200 | (10, 1024) |
| wheel and crank data | `30 00 c8 00 01 00 00 00 02 00 0a 00 00 04` | 200 | (10, 1024) |
| torque, wheel and crank data | `34 00 c8 00 07 00 01 00 00 00 02 00 0a 00 00 04` | 200 | (10, 1024) |
| no crank data | `00 00 c8 00` | 200 | - |
| negative power | `00 00 fb ff` | -5 | - |

### 6.2 Cadence from crank data

`rpm = (dr*60*1024*2 + dt) // (2*dt)` with `dr`, `dt` the u16 differences modulo 65536. No new event (`dr == 0` or `dt == 0`): keep the last value for 3 s of silence, then show 0.

| previous | current | rpm |
|---|---|---|
| (10, 1024) | (13, 3072) | 90 |
| (65535, 65000) | (2, 1000) | 120 |
| (100, 5000) | (101, 5683) | 90 |
| (100, 5000) | (100, 5000) | none |
| (0, 0) | (1, 400) | 154 |

### 6.3 Heart Rate Measurement (0x2A37)

| bytes | bpm |
|---|---|
| `00 48` | 72 |
| `00 ff` | 255 |
| `01 b4 00` | 180 |
| `01 2c 01` | 300 |

### 6.4 One-second bins

A bin is the half-up integer mean of the samples received in that second: `(sum*2 + n) // (2*n)`. An empty second takes the last value if it is 2000 ms old or less, otherwise the bin is invalid (engineering brief 5.1). A valid 0 is a number.

| samples in the second | bin |
|---|---|
| [200] | 200 |
| [200, 203] | 202 |
| [199, 200] | 200 |
| [0, 0] | 0 |
| [251, 252, 254] | 252 |
| (none) | none (stale rule applies) |

## 7. Workout status pill

Input: one 3 s average power per second against the step range (here 247 to 260 W). It leaves IN RANGE only after 2 consecutive seconds outside on the same side; it returns to IN RANGE at once. `delta` is the distance to the nearest edge while HIGH or LOW.

| t | power | state | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 255 | IN | 0 |
| 1 | 262 | IN | 0 |
| 2 | 263 | HIGH | 3 |
| 3 | 264 | HIGH | 4 |
| 4 | 258 | IN | 0 |
| 5 | 245 | IN | 0 |
| 6 | 244 | LOW | 3 |
| 7 | 243 | LOW | 4 |
| 8 | 250 | IN | 0 |
| 9 | 261 | IN | 0 |
| 10 | 245 | IN | 0 |
| 11 | 270 | IN | 0 |
| 12 | 271 | HIGH | 11 |
| 13 | 240 | LOW | 7 |

Read t=9 to t=11: a single second above the range followed by a second below it does not leave IN RANGE, because the 2 s must be on the same side. t=13 shows an immediate switch from HIGH to LOW once already out of range.

### 7.1 Step start and short steps

At each step start the pill resets and is hidden for the first 5 s; the section 7 rules then apply from elapsed second 5, with the 2 s count starting there. On a step shorter than 60 s it is hidden throughout. Below, a 480 s step with range 247 to 260 W entered from a recovery, so the 3 s power is still climbing at the start. | 1 | 190 | hidden | - | LOW |
| 2 | 230 | hidden | - | LOW |
| 3 | 252 | hidden | - | IN |
| 4 | 262 | hidden | - | IN |

| 6 | 265 | HIGH | 5 | HIGH |

## 8. Climb detection (phone)

Input: elevation in metres at a fixed spacing along the route. Defaults (provisional, all adjustable in the app): hysteresis 5 m, merge when the dip is at most 8 m and the gap at most 150 m, keep a climb only if length is at least 300 m, ascent at least 15 m and average grade at least 3.0 %. Output `avg_x10` and `max_x10` are grades in tenths of a percent; the max uses a 50 m window.

Synthetic profile: 256 samples at 10 m. 50 samples flat at 20 m, 60 samples rising 0.5 m each (5 %), a 6 m dip over 6 samples, 40 samples rising 0.6 m each (6 %), 50 samples falling 0.4 m each, 30 flat, then 20 samples rising 0.3 m each (a 6 m bump).

```
[{"start_m": 490, "length_m": 1060, "ascent_m": 48, "avg_x10": 45, "max_x10": 60}]
```

The two climbs separated by the 6 m dip merge into one, starting at the last flat sample (490 m), not at the start of the flat. Ascent is net gain from start to summit (68 - 20 = 48 m). The final bump (6 m gain) is rejected on ascent. The full test set also includes: a profile that never exceeds 2 % (no climbs), a single steep 200 m ramp of 12 m (rejected, below 300 m and 15 m), and a flat profile (no climbs). Add these as unit tests; they need no golden numbers.

## 10. How to use this file

Port the cases into the unit test suites (firmware host tests, Kotlin unit tests, `tools/` Python tests). The section 4 tables are the validation test list; sections 2 and 5 are golden bytes; sections 6 to 8 pin the algorithms. When a threshold marked provisional changes in the spec, regenerate this file from the reference implementation rather than editing numbers by hand.
